Dawkins for Customer Success

An AI assistant that drafts every reply, a person reviews it before it ships, and the assistant gets smarter every time someone corrects it. No engineer needed to teach it.

The idea in one paragraph

A customer asks a question. Dawkins (our AI) writes a draft reply using the customer's question plus everything the team has corrected before. A real CS person reads the draft and either approves it, edits it, or rewrites it. Whatever the human ends up sending is saved as a lesson — so the next time a similar question comes in, Dawkins drafts a better reply on its own. The CS team teaches the assistant just by doing their job.

The dotted yellow arrows are the part that's new: every approval, edit, or rewrite becomes a memory the AI uses on the next question. The team trains it by working — no engineer required.

What changes for the CS team

1Day one

Dawkins drafts replies based on help-center docs. Drafts are okay but generic. The team mostly edits before sending.

2Week two

Drafts now match the team's actual tone and structure. Approve rate climbs because past edits became lessons.

3Month one

Common questions become one-click approvals. CS time shifts from typing replies to handling the genuinely hard tickets.

4Always

A human ships every reply. The AI never reaches the customer alone. Quality bar = whatever the team accepts.
